Per-event spread risk

Every active fire event gets a live weather reading and a one-word risk label — moderate, elevated, high or extreme — because a smouldering 8 MW hotspot in 39% humidity with 32 km/h gusts is a different proposition from the same hotspot in still, damp air. This is enrich.py, the oldest and simplest of FireWatch's three weather-driven features.

01 What this is

Coordinates and an FRP number are not a notification. This is what turns “44.31, 18.29, 8.4 MW” into something a reader can act on.

Once a cycle, for every event whose status is active (poller.py's enrich step), enrich.weather() asks Open-Meteo for the current conditions at that exact point, and enrich.fire_risk() turns the result into one of four words. Quiet events are skipped entirely — a fire nobody expects to spread today does not need a live weather call.

Not a validated index

Unlike the fire danger forecast, which ports a peer-reviewed, internationally standard algorithm, this is a small hand-written heuristic specific to this project — four thresholds on gust speed and humidity, chosen to be defensible, not derived from a published fire behaviour model. Treat the label as a rough, current-conditions flag, not a rated danger score.

02 The four tiers

The whole function is nine lines. Gust speed falls back to plain wind speed when Open-Meteo reports no gust figure; a missing humidity reading is treated as 100% (i.e. it can never push risk up on its own).

The swatches match what the map popup actually does with each tier — see §05: only high and extreme are drawn in colour. moderate and elevated render identically, in neutral grey.

03 Why AND, then OR

The joining word changes exactly once, between high and extreme, and that is not an accident of how the conditions happened to be written.

elevated and high use OR: either strong wind or low humidity alone is enough, because either one independently makes a fire harder to contain — wind by driving spread rate, dry air by drying fuel. A fire in a 32 km/h gust and 60% humidity is still meaningfully more dangerous than one in calm air, humidity aside; the OR is what lets wind speak for itself.

extreme uses AND: both conditions have to be severe at once. That is a deliberate ceiling on how easily the top label fires. Given the same thresholds joined by OR instead, any single very gusty afternoon (≥40 km/h, common with an approaching front, regardless of humidity) would read as “extreme” — the label that reaches an SMS, worded to the strongest word this system has (§05). Requiring both keeps that word for conditions that are actually compounding, not just windy.

04 Data source and caching

Weather comes from Open-Meteo (api.open-meteo.com/v1/forecast, current= fields), free and keyless — the same host the fire danger forecast uses, queried independently and for a different purpose. Temperature, humidity, wind speed, wind direction and gusts come back for the event's exact coordinates, timezone Europe/Sarajevo.

Results are cached in memory for 15 minutes, keyed on the coordinates rounded to two decimal places (≈1.1 km at this latitude) — so two fires close enough to round to the same key, or the same fire polled across a few consecutive cycles, share one Open-Meteo call rather than repeating it. A failed lookup returns None and the event simply carries no weather or risk that cycle, rather than breaking the poll.

05 Where it shows up

Four independent consumers read ev["risk"]; none of them recompute it.

Map popup (mapgen.py)

In the event card's weather line. Rendered in red only for high/ extreme; moderate/elevated stay the same grey as the surrounding text. Bilingual — the map's EN/BS toggle translates the word itself.

Menu bar (menubar.py)

A plain Spread risk: high line under an active fire's submenu, always in English regardless of sms_language.

CLI report (__main__.py)

python3 -m firewatch poll/status print … high spread risk alongside each active fire's weather line.

SMS alert (sms.py)

The fourth and last line of an active-fire text: Wind 32km/h SZ RH 28% rizik visok. The one consumer where this is not just informational — it spends part of the 160-character, one-segment budget.

The SMS line is not optional padding

Per this project's own measurement, every active-fire alert already carries weather, and the message is verified to fit one GSM-7 segment (160 chars) at its worst case across every stored event, every alert kind, and the longest settlement name. The risk word is part of that measured budget — sms.worst_case() is what re-checks it, not a hand count.

06 vs. the fire danger forecast

Both features call the same weather API for the same municipality. They answer different questions, on different clocks, at different rigour.
